Why Baggage Rules Feel Like a Trap

Almost every frequent flyer has a version of the same story. The bag has flown a dozen times without trouble. The trip is booked. Packing is done in twenty minutes flat. Then, at the gate, a staff member points at a metal frame and asks you to step over and try your suitcase inside it. It does not fit. The fee that follows is rarely small, and it almost always lands at the worst possible moment — right before boarding, when there is no real way to negotiate.

Airline baggage policy is not one rule. It is a stack of rules that change depending on which airline is flying the plane, which fare you bought, how many flights are on your ticket, and — increasingly — which gate agent is standing in front of you that day. The International Air Transport Association says this plainly on its own passenger baggage page: baggage rules are set by individual airlines, not by any global authority. There is no universal carry-on size.

What has changed in the last few years is enforcement. Airlines that once waved bags through on a busy boarding queue now measure more bags, more often, with physical sizer frames at the gate. A bag that travelled fine before is not a guarantee it travels fine on a new airline or route today.

Add a second flight — a connection, a return on a different carrier, a booking where the plane you board is operated by a regional partner you never heard of — and the comparison becomes genuinely difficult to run in your head. Each leg can have a different size limit, a different weight limit, and a different idea of what counts as a personal item. That is the gap BagFit is built to close.

Step-by-Step: Your First Check

Add your bags — or pick a preset

Start by choosing the bag type (personal item, carry-on, or checked bag), then enter the three packed dimensions and weight. You can enter in centimetres and kilograms, or switch to inches and pounds — BagFit converts everything internally. If you are not sure of exact measurements yet, the new quick-preset dropdown gives you nine common sizes to start from: standard EU carry-on (55×40×23 cm), US domestic carry-on (22×14×9 in), compact personal item, large checked bag, and more. Once you have entered or chosen measurements, BagFit remembers them automatically in your browser so the next visit starts where you left off.

Set the bag options

Tick whether the dimensions already include wheels and handles (they should — that is how a gate sizer measures it), whether the bag is soft-sided, and whether an expandable section is open. These change the comparison in a meaningful way. The soft-sided flag activates the small compression grace where it genuinely applies; the expandable flag ensures BagFit uses the true packed size rather than the bag’s closed minimum.

Add each flight

For every flight on your itinerary, choose the marketing airline (the one you booked with) and — whenever possible — the operating airline (the one actually flying the plane). These can differ on codeshare or regional partner flights, and it is the operating airline’s rules that apply at the gate. Also choose the cabin class and the fare type. Since version 7.9, Premium Economy is a selectable fare type alongside Basic Economy, Standard, and Flexible — making the comparison accurate for travellers on premium fares with different allowances.

Enter the checked-bag allowance from your ticket

For checked bags, paste or type the allowance line straight from your booking confirmation — something like “1PC 23KG” or “2×23KG 158CM”. BagFit can now read three-dimensional formats (55×40×20), the shorthand “23K” for kilograms, and keywords like BAGS, PIECES, and HOLD alongside the standard formats. Once it reads the values, check them against your actual ticket before running the comparison.

Run the check and read the Decision Board

The result appears as a Flight Decision Board: one clear verdict at the top, then the exact bag and exact flight responsible for it, then the specific dimension or weight that pushed things over. Open any bag or any flight leg to see the full breakdown. The Every Bag section shows a best-orientation row — the optimal way to pack your bag into the overhead bin for the tightest rule on your trip.

Tip — measure the packed bag, not the empty one: fill the bag the way you plan to travel, then measure the outside including any compression straps, pocket bumps, wheels, handles, and feet. A bag that measures 54 cm empty can easily read 57 cm when packed, which is the number that matters at the gate.

Understanding Every Verdict

BagFit uses specific, honest verdict codes rather than a generic yes/no. Here is what each one means for you as a traveller.

✓ FITS Your bag’s size and weight both clear the airline’s stated limit with room to spare, and the booking allowance is confirmed. This is a clean pass — no condition attached.
! FITS WITH CONDITIONS The physical measurements pass, but something about your fare or booking still needs a quick confirmation — usually whether your specific ticket type actually includes that bag.
⌛ SOURCE STALE The bag fits the stored numbers, but the data for this airline is older than the recommended review window. Confirm the current official policy before you fly. New in v7.9.
✗ DOES NOT FIT One or more bags exceed a confirmed size or weight limit. The exact dimension or weight overrun is shown — you know precisely what needs to change and by how much.
? CHECK REQUIRED BagFit does not have a precise enough number for this airline and bag type to make a comparison. The tool tells you exactly which detail to ask the airline about directly.
✗ NOT INCLUDED The bag may fit physically, but the selected fare or allowance option does not include it in the price. You would need to add it to your booking before check-in.

Below the headline verdict, every result also shows a percentage — how close each bag is running to the limit on size and weight separately. A bag at 60% of the weight limit and 95% of the size limit is a very different problem than one near the edge on both, and the display shows you which dimension actually needs attention.

How Airlines Actually Enforce Their Own Rules

The published limit and the real-world enforcement are not always the same thing. Some airlines weigh and measure carry-on bags at almost every gate with a physical sizer frame that allows no exceptions. Others post the same numbers on their website but rarely check them on quieter routes or at older terminals.

BagFit attaches an enforcement signal — strictly enforced, selectively enforced, or rarely enforced — to airlines where this is genuinely documented, along with a specific note about how that airline tends to check: at the counter, at the boarding gate, on certain hub airports more than others.

This is context, not a loophole: the enforcement signal changes how carefully you should treat a bag that’s borderline. It is not an invitation to pack over the limit and hope for the best. A bag running 5% over on an airline with lenient enforcement is a different risk from the same bag on a carrier using automated gate scanners at every boarding call — but both are risks.

Basic Economy, Premium Economy and the Fare Traps Nobody Reads Closely

One of the most expensive baggage mistakes has nothing to do with measuring a bag wrong. It is assuming that “Economy” means the same baggage allowance regardless of which specific fare you bought. On several major airlines it very much does not.

One large U.S. carrier restricts its cheapest domestic fare to a personal item only — no included carry-on — with a real gate fee if an oversized bag shows up anyway. Others are more generous with the bag itself but move Basic Economy passengers to the last boarding group, by which point the overhead bins are often already full regardless of what the ticket technically allows.

BagFit’s fare type field handles this properly. Selecting your fare surfaces the exact rule for that specific fare rather than the generic Economy answer that would otherwise apply. Since version 7.9, Premium Economy is a proper selectable option — meaning travellers on those fares (which often include a larger cabin allowance, an extra personal item, or priority boarding) now get an accurate comparison instead of being lumped in with Standard Economy.

Not sure which fare you bought? Leave the fare type as “Not sure” and BagFit will flag that the comparison may not match your specific ticket — and prompt you to check the fare confirmation before running a more precise result.

Soft Bags, Compression and Why “It Fit Last Time” Is Getting Riskier

Soft-sided bags behave differently from hard-shell suitcases at the actual gate. A fabric backpack or duffel can compress slightly to fit a sizer frame in a way a rigid case cannot, and BagFit’s soft-sided toggle accounts for this — but only where it genuinely applies.

The compression grace is small and precise: 5 mm per dimension, applied only on airlines with documented low enforcement where manual checking is used rather than automated scanners. This was corrected in version 7.9 from a previous value that was ten times too large (50 mm — nearly 2 inches per side — which was passing bags that were clearly over the limit).

On airlines using automated gate scanners, a soft bag gets exactly the same treatment as a hard one. The tool reflects that honestly rather than giving every soft bag the same generous discount regardless of how the airline checks it. That is also why “it fit last time” is becoming a less reliable guide — a route that used a manual check in previous years may now use a sizer frame, and the same bag that was waved through before may be stopped today.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Voyasee BagFit free to use?

Yes. There is no account requirement and no paywall on the checker itself. The site supports itself through the affiliate links described above and standard advertising — not through charging for the tool.

Does it remember my bag measurements between visits?

Yes, from version 7.9. BagFit saves your bag measurements automatically in your browser’s local storage. The next time you open the tool, your bags are already filled in. This uses only your own device — no account, no server storage of measurements.

What are the quick preset bag sizes?

Version 7.9 adds a quick-preset dropdown with nine common sizes: three personal item options (40×30×15 cm, 45×35×20 cm, 18×14×8 in), four carry-on options (55×40×23 cm EU standard, 56×45×25 cm EU large, 55×35×20 cm low-cost compact, 22×14×9 in US domestic), and two checked bag options (68×45×27 cm and 78×52×31 cm). Select a preset and the dimensions fill in automatically. You can then adjust any dimension to match your actual bag.

What is SOURCE STALE and why does it matter?

SOURCE STALE is a verdict that appears when your bag fits the stored size and weight numbers, but the data for that airline has not been reviewed recently enough to trust confidently. Previous versions would silently downgrade this to a generic conditional pass without explaining why. The stale verdict is now explicit so you know exactly which detail to go confirm before you fly — rather than discovering at the airport that the rule changed since the data was collected.

What does “best orientation” mean in the result?

BagFit tests all six ways a rectangular bag can be loaded into an overhead bin or sizer frame (rotating it across all three axes). The best-orientation row in the Every Bag section shows the specific arrangement — length × width × depth — that gives your bag its best chance of fitting within the tightest stored limit on your trip. This tells you not just whether the bag passes, but how to pack it into the bin to take advantage of that pass.

Can I check Premium Economy and Basic Economy separately?

Yes. The fare type field offers Not sure, Basic Economy, Standard / Main, Premium Economy (new in v7.9), and Flexible / Refundable. Selecting Premium Economy surfaces the exact allowance for that fare tier on airlines where it differs from Standard Economy. Selecting Basic Economy flags any known restriction for that fare — on several major carriers, Basic Economy removes the included carry-on entirely.

Does it check checked baggage or only carry-on?

Both, along with the personal item category separately. For checked bags the tool is more conservative by design — where your ticket already specifies an allowance, enter it directly for the most accurate result rather than relying on a general airline default.

How does the ticket allowance text parser work?

Paste or type the allowance line from your booking — for example “1PC 23KG”, “2×23KG 158CM”, “55×40×20 CM”, or “23K”. BagFit reads piece count, weight in kg or lb (with automatic conversion), linear total in cm or inches, and three-dimensional sizes (computing the linear total from all three). It also reads shorthand formats like “23K” for kilograms. Always check the extracted values against your actual ticket before running the comparison.

Can I check a trip with a connecting flight on a different airline?

Yes, and this is one of the most useful parts of the tool. Add each flight separately with its own marketing airline, operating airline, cabin and fare type. The result identifies which specific leg is the strictest one for each bag, rather than giving you one blended answer for the whole trip.

What if the operating airline differs from the one I booked?

Use the operating airline field for the carrier actually flying the aircraft. The rules that apply at the gate are the operating carrier’s rules, not the marketing airline’s. If you do not know the operating airline, tick the codeshare-unknown box and BagFit will add a notice to confirm this before flying.

How accurate is the data for less common airlines?

The tool tells you which confidence tier each airline falls into. Fifty major airlines are individually deep-verified against their current official baggage page. Mid-size carriers carry numbers from official sources with a lower confidence flag. Smaller regional airlines without a clear published number are listed with a direct link to their own page and no invented dimensions attached.

Can I save and share my result with other travellers?

Yes. Results can be saved (generating a shareable link that renders the same result for whoever opens it), printed as a two-page document with the full breakdown, or stored automatically in your browser between visits. The printed version includes official source links and the airport staff script.

Does using this tool mean I do not need to check with the airline?

No. Every result links to the airline’s own official baggage page specifically so you can confirm it yourself before flying. Gate staff have the final say regardless of what any third-party tool shows. The goal is to walk into the airport already knowing the answer — not to replace the airline’s own rules entirely.

Does it work on a phone?

It was built mobile-first on the reasonable assumption that most people are packing a bag and checking a phone in the same room. The full multi-bag, multi-flight check, the Decision Board, the preset dropdown, and the printable result all work on a phone screen the same way as on a laptop. Dark mode also works automatically on phones that have it enabled.

Does BagFit support dark mode?

Yes, from version 7.9. BagFit detects your device or browser’s dark-mode preference via the standard prefers-color-scheme media query and switches to a full dark colour scheme automatically. No toggle or setting is needed — it follows your system preference.

What if airline data fails to load?

If the airline data cannot be fetched (for example due to a temporary connection issue), a Retry button appears immediately so you can re-attempt without refreshing the whole page or re-entering your bag measurements.
